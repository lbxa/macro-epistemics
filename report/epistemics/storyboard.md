# Macro-Epistemics

## A two-minute research film — storyboard v4

**Runtime:** 1:58, including opening, transitions and closing hold. Hard cap: 2:00.  
**Format:** 16:9; compose for 1920 × 1080, with vector artwork and smooth motion.  
**Audience:** a curious person who cares about the future of AI and has never heard of Veracity.  
**Treatment:** a visionary product reveal grounded in a real research experiment; ivory and cream, dark greens, earthy accents, and a soft, beautiful melody.  
**Production stage:** storyboard for later implementation with `anim`.  
**Project and film title:** Macro-Epistemics.  
**Tagline:** Make AI Answerable.  
**Explanatory subtitle:** How AI claims hold up across domains.  
**Research instrument:** Veracity. The accompanying paper remains *What Models Defend: Claim Revision and Jury Agreement*.  
**Research credit:** Lucas Barbosa, NYU · Stefan Gazzana, Roundarc.  
**Event:** Apart Research · AI x Epistemics Research Sprint · November 2026.

**The story in one sentence:** Imagine a future in which people can challenge consequential AI claims, inspect the evidence, and learn from visible corrections; Macro-Epistemics studies how to make that possible, beginning with Veracity's claim-and-defence experiments.

The emotional progression is personal possibility → anticipation → revelation → discovery → a shared future. The desired response is: **“I understand the idea, I see why it matters, and I want to see this exist.”**

## What we are unveiling

The promise is **a way to question the answers shaping our lives**. Macro-Epistemics is the umbrella project: studying how the reliability of AI claims changes across tasks, domains and evaluators, and ultimately what those signals mean for people and institutions. Veracity is the experimental instrument: save what a model said, challenge a claim, let it defend or revise, and check what happened against independent evidence. One surprising pilot result makes the idea tangible. The final act opens the horizon to a shared memory of claims, evidence and corrections that people and institutions could inspect.

Use keynote principles: reveal a consequential possibility, give the idea a name, explain one memorable interaction, show one result that changes the viewer's understanding, then widen the horizon. Give the audience time to want the idea before introducing the experiment. The reveal uses our own voice and original language.

The ambition can be large while its status stays explicit. **Pilot finding** identifies observed evidence. **Research direction** identifies the broader study. **Vision** identifies the imagined human benefit. The horizon is a hypothesis about what this work could enable, rather than a claim that we have solved trust, truth or governance.

## The first ten seconds

**Chosen hook:**

> What if you could challenge the AI shaping your future—and see which answers actually stand up?

Start on the human consequence, with a question already in motion. The visual changes from a recommendation flowing toward a person into a claim opening to reveal its evidence. That transformation is the promise of the entire project in miniature. Reveal **Macro-Epistemics** at second ten, with the plain-language tagline **Make AI Answerable**.

The hook should work with sound off: **Your health. Your livelihood. Our future.** becomes **What if you could challenge the answer?** Keep the words large and the composition spare. A quiet caption, **Imagine…**, places these consequential applications in the envisioned future. Use the entire first ten seconds for that promise.

## Visual and musical direction

Take inspiration from 3Blue1Brown's explanatory choreography: generous negative space, precise shapes, gentle transformations and diagrams that develop as the explanation develops. Set those ideas on a luminous ivory-and-cream canvas with deep forest-green typography and earthy botanical colors, following Lucas's direction for the Apart Research identity. The feeling is a beautifully typeset research notebook: warm paper, confident ink, delicate diagrams and quiet depth.

The central visual object is a **cream answer card with a forest-green corner notch**. It starts as a recommendation, opens to reveal evidence, becomes the saved claim in our experiment, then joins an imagined network of inspectable claims and corrections. Its growing connections are drawn as precise, gently organic lines. This single object carries the film from a personal question to a human-scale vision.

## Scene 3 — Make the interaction tangible

**Picture and motion**

## Scene 5 — The possibility of correction

**00:57–01:12 · 15 seconds**

**Narration**

> On one saved error, DeepSeek corrected itself in one of five reviews—and all five source audits. We're looking for the conditions that help models correct themselves.

**Picture and motion**

- **00:57–01:02:** Introduce a DeepSeek saved-answer card on the same error case. It branches into two displayed conditions, **Review** and **Source audit**. The label **Same original answer · same agreement** spans both paths.
- **01:02–01:08:** Five seed marks appear for each condition in their actual order. A moss circle is a correction; a burnt-clay cross is a remaining error. The counts settle at **1/5** and **5/5**, respectively.
- **01:08–01:12:** A sage underline crosses an existing source passage. One corrected answer reconnects to its evidence. Hold the line **Scrutiny can lead to revision.** A small header says **Observed on this saved error**.

**Direction:** This is one contrast within a four-condition, five-seed pilot. The audit changes attention and reasoning guidance over the same agreement. It supplies no new external fact, and this result does not isolate a Veracity platform effect. Preserve actual seed identities; the jury's seven reference disagreements never morph into correction data.

**Sound and transition:** The melody opens a little as the correction path reconnects. Treat the correction as an encouraging observation with remaining questions. The two five-seed groups come together beneath a single document bracket.

## Editorial choices and production handoff

The film's goal is to make people understand and want the possibility of contestable AI claims. It moves from a human consequence to the named research idea, an understandable interaction, two pilot observations and an ambitious shared future. The completed legal pilot supplies its evidence; the wider vision supplies its invitation. Keep the concrete result and the hoped-for human benefit visually distinct.

The conversation began with finance infrastructure and synthetic-stakes comparisons. Those earlier runs, API billing and historical collusion campaigns remain supporting context. The broader sector campaign is a separate study whose evolving outcomes require their own admission review. This cut deliberately uses the already admitted pilot findings; it does not pool protocols or present the expansion's design as results.

The main sprint question is the assertion–defence gap. The distinction between factual confidence and jury forecasts points toward calibration; the open decision tile names the still-unmeasured decision-quality question. The seven accepted reference-incorrect originals expose a verdict-only scoring risk relevant to benchmark maintenance. The film does not claim an intentional exploit, a closed loophole, or a validated calibration benchmark.

For the later `anim` implementation, use a small visual vocabulary: human figure, answer card, evidence document, branching path, seed symbol, jury ring, reference badge and correction mark. Their transformations carry the viewer from one person to a conceptual shared network. Keep visible quotations distinct from schematic labels, preserve labels when colors change, and leave unresolved paths visible in the visionary network.

Before production, test the opening ten seconds independently, both with sound and muted. The viewer should understand the human stake and the possibility of challenging an answer before the Macro-Epistemics reveal. The following scene must distinguish the project from its instrument, Veracity. Then read the entire narration aloud with its visual holds. Keep the contractual excerpt short and the data settled while it is read. If a line needs more time, shorten it rather than accelerating the voice or exceeding 2:00.
