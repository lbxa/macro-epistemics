\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}
\textbf{Benchmark work as an evidential anchor.}
FinQA~\cite{chen2021} and TAT-QA~\cite{zhu2021} ground financial calculations
in reports and tables. ContractNLI~\cite{koreeda2021} provides contract
inference labels, SciFact~\cite{wadden2020} scientific claims with annotated
evidence, and MathDial~\cite{macina2023} mathematical tutoring dialogues.
Our disputes reuse these sources; scalar and categorical grading excludes
broader outcomes such as MathDial's pedagogical quality.

\textbf{Confidence and answer revision.}
Kadavath et al.~\cite{kadavath2022} distinguish answer validity from
answerability; Tian et al.~\cite{tian2023} evaluate elicited probabilities.
Sycophancy~\cite{sharma2024} and limitations of self-correction without
external feedback~\cite{huang2024} motivate our pressure, review and audit
controls.

\textbf{Evaluating the evaluator.}
LLM-as-a-judge studies document agreement with humans and systematic
biases~\cite{zheng2023}, including response-order effects~\cite{wang2024},
prompt-complexity sensitivity and leniency~\cite{thakur2025}.
CALM~\cite{ye2024} tests biases through controlled modifications. Our grading
audit and cross-family replay test whether consensus preserves errors.

\textbf{Argumentation and verification.}
Khan et al.~\cite{khan2024} find that opposing expert arguments help weaker
judges on reading comprehension. We instead test authors' responses to
controlled objections and juries' acceptance of the original claim. The
numerical extension varies defence and checked-calculation visibility.
Barbosa et al.~\cite{barbosa2025veracity} propose a mechanism in which
creators and challengers stake collateral and a jury rules on the dispute.

\textbf{Benchmark validity.}
LiveBench refreshes questions to reduce contamination~\cite{white2024}; our
audit examines answer leakage through generated objections. Unlike
$\tau$-bench's final-database checks~\cite{yao2024}, our text tasks do not verify
completed professional actions.
