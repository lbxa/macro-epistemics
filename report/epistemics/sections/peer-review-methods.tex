\subsection{Targeted replications after the primary study}
\label{sec:peer-review-methods}

Two separately versioned extensions preserve the primary observations.
The numerical rerun derives alternatives from public answers, with references
only vetoing matches. This blocks the demonstrated encoding but retains an
oracle dependency. It reuses 60 sources, five authors, frozen originals,
seeds and the GPT-OSS settlement protocol. Collection times differ, so the
legacy comparison is descriptive rather than a randomized policy effect.

The judge replication selects four errors and four correct controls from
FinQA and MathDial, including the historical arithmetic trigger. New cases
have audit defences supporting their originals; selection does not use jury
outcomes. Controls match benchmarks, not authors or difficulty.
DeepSeek and GPT-OSS receive claim and objection alone, with defence, or with
defence and a checked calculation. Five seeds and three calls per cell yield
720 standalone judgments, capped at 2,048 output tokens. Failures remain;
a sensitivity analysis excludes the trigger.

Exact arithmetic validates proof cards; operators review source interpretation
and withhold ambiguous cases. Human adjudication remains pending
(Appendix~\ref{app:reference-adjudication}). Checked evidence supplies derivation
and answer, confounding verification with trust. Selected cases cannot
estimate prevalence.

Paired contrasts follow Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}, matching judge call
indices and separating controls from errors. Missing-outcome bounds hold
observed votes fixed, let missing votes range from zero to one, then average
all assignments within source. These are identification bounds, not confidence
intervals. Appendix~\ref{app:peer-review} gives completion and provenance.
